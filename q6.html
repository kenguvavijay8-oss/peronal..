ENDANGERED EXOTIC ANIMALS

VAQUITA

1. Scientific Classification

Scientific Name: Phocoena sinus
Category: Marine Mammal
Family: Phocoenidae

2. Physical Description

The Vaquita is the smallest and rarest porpoise, growing about 1.4–1.5 meters long and weighing around 50–55 kg. It has a robust, rounded body with dark rings around its eyes, dark patches on the lips, a light gray to dark gray body, a lighter belly, and a small dorsal fin.

3. Habitat & Distribution

Found only in the northern Gulf of California, Mexico, in shallow, warm coastal waters. It has the smallest distribution of any marine mammal and is critically endangered due to bycatch in gillnets used for fishing.

Speciality

  • World’s rarest marine mammal, with fewer than 20 individuals estimated in the wild.
  • Small size and shy behavior make it very difficult to observe.
  • Vital for maintaining the marine ecosystem balance in the Gulf of California.
  • Conservation efforts focus on removing gillnets and protecting habitat.
  • Sometimes called the “panda of the sea” because of its rarity and black facial markings.

COELACANTH

1. Scientific Classification

Scientific Name: Latimeria chalumnae
Category: Lobe-finned Fish
Family: Latimeriidae

2. Physical Description

Coelacanths are ancient lobe-finned fish, growing up to 2 meters in length and weighing up to 90 kg. They have a thick, elongated bluish-gray body with white spots, paired lobed fins that move in a leg-like fashion, a three-lobed tail, a hinged skull for wide feeding gape, and rough cosmoid scales.

3. Habitat & Distribution

Deep-sea dwellers found at depths of 150–700 meters, living in caves and rocky underwater ledges in warm ocean waters. Found in the Indian Ocean near Comoros, Madagascar, South Africa, and in Indonesian waters. Once thought extinct for 65 million years until rediscovered in 1938.

Speciality

  • Considered a “living fossil” with little change for over 400 million years.
  • Lobe-finned structure is an evolutionary link between fish and first land vertebrates.
  • Can survive in deep, low-oxygen environments.
  • Extremely rare and critically endangered.
  • Rediscovery challenged long-held beliefs about extinction and evolution.

snow leopord

1. Scientific Classification

Scientific Name: Panthera uncia
Category: Mammal
Family: Felidae

2. Physical Description

Snow leopards are medium-sized big cats, weighing 22–55 kg and measuring 90–120 cm in body length, with a tail of 80–100 cm. They have thick, smoky-gray fur with black rosettes and spots, large powerful paws, a long thick tail, and small rounded ears adapted to cold mountainous environments.

3. Habitat & Distribution

Native to mountain ranges of Central and South Asia, including the Himalayas, Tibet, Mongolia, and parts of Russia. They prefer rocky, rugged terrains at high altitudes (3,000–4,500 meters), with cliffs and ridges for hunting. Endangered due to poaching, habitat loss, and declining prey populations.

Speciality

  • Exceptionally adapted to cold, high-altitude environments.
  • Can jump up to 15 meters, useful for hunting on steep cliffs.
  • Solitary and elusive, very difficult to spot in the wild.
  • Thick fur and long tail provide insulation against subzero temperatures.
  • Plays a key role in controlling herbivore populations in its ecosystem.
