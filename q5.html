Amphibians

AXOLOTL

An axolotl is a neotenic amphibian native to the lakes of Mexico, especially Lake Xochimilco. Unlike most amphibians, axolotls remain in their larval (juvenile) form throughout their entire life, meaning they keep their gills, fins, and aquatic lifestyle even as adults. They are also known as Mexican walking fish, though they are not fish—they are amphibians.

1. Scientific Classification

Scientific Name: Ambystoma mexicanum
Category: Amphibian
Family: Ambystomatidae

2. Physical Description

Axolotls typically measure 15–30 cm long and have a wide head with lidless eyes. They retain external feathery gills on both sides of the head and a dorsal fin along the tail. Color forms include wild-type (dark with speckles), leucistic (pale pink with red gills), albino, golden, and melanoid.

3. Habitat & Distribution

Native to Mexico, axolotls are found in the remaining canals and waterways of Lake Xochimilco. They live in cool, freshwater environments with dense vegetation. Wild populations have declined dramatically due to habitat loss, pollution, and invasive species.

Speciality

  • Exhibit extreme regeneration — they can regrow limbs, tail, spinal cord, and parts of organs.
  • Neotenic: they retain juvenile features (gills and aquatic lifestyle) throughout life.
  • Important in scientific research for studying tissue regeneration and developmental biology.
  • Critically endangered in the wild but common in controlled captive populations.
  • Distinctive "smiling" appearance and feathery gills make them popular in education and as research animals.

CHINESE GIANT SALAMENDER

The Chinese giant salamander is the largest amphibian in the world, capable of reaching lengths of up to 1.8 meters. It belongs to the family Cryptobranchidae and is native to the mountain streams and rivers of China. This species has a broad head, small eyes, wrinkled skin, and a flat body adapted for aquatic life.

1. Scientific Classification

Scientific Name: Andrias davidianus
Category: Amphibian
Family: Cryptobranchidae

2. Physical Description

The Chinese giant salamander is the largest amphibian in the world, reaching up to 1.8 meters in length and weighing over 50 kg. It has a broad, flat head, small lidless eyes, and wrinkled, loose skin that helps absorb oxygen. Its flat body, short limbs, and long tail are adapted for aquatic life. Skin color ranges from dark brown to black.

3. Habitat & Distribution

Native to cool, fast-flowing mountain streams and rivers in China. Prefers rocky environments with hiding spots. Wild populations have declined drastically due to water pollution, habitat destruction, and overharvesting for food and traditional medicine.

Speciality

  • Largest living amphibian on Earth.
  • Can live over 50 years in captivity.
  • Considered a living fossil, with ancestors dating back more than 170 million years.
  • Fully aquatic and breathes through both skin and lungs.
  • Top predator in freshwater ecosystems, playing an important ecological role.

KIHANSI SPARY TOAD

The Kihansi Spray Toad (Nectophrynoides asperginis) is a critically endangered amphibian species that was native to a very small area in the Kihansi Gorge of Tanzania. It is named for its habitat in the spray zone of waterfalls, where constant mist keeps the environment moist.

1. Scientific Classification

Scientific Name: Nectophrynoides asperginis
Category: Amphibian
Family: Bufonidae

2. Physical Description

The Kihansi Spray Toad is very small, about 2–3 cm long, with yellowish-brown skin and darker markings for camouflage. It has a smooth, compact body and short but strong limbs, adapted to cling to wet rocks and vegetation in its humid waterfall spray habitat.

3. Habitat & Distribution

Native to Kihansi Gorge, Tanzania, near the Kihansi Falls. Lives in the spray zone of waterfalls, where constant mist maintains high humidity. The natural habitat was drastically altered due to a hydroelectric dam, leading to extinction in the wild. Survives now through captive breeding programs and reintroduction efforts.

Speciality

  • Females give birth to fully formed young instead of laying eggs.
  • Extremely small and habitat-specific, highly vulnerable to environmental changes.
  • Only found in a tiny natural range, making it one of the rarest amphibians.
  • Depends entirely on the humid microhabitat created by waterfall spray.
  • Conservation success story through captive breeding programs for potential reintroduction.
